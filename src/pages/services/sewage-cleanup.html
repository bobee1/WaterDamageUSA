Quick answer: Sewage cleanup removes sewer water and waste, throws out soaked porous materials, and cleans, disinfects and dries every surface it touched. Sewage is Category 3 (black) water under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, so the crew works in protective gear. Keep people and pets out of the area and don't run fans until the sewage is removed.

What sewage cleanup includes

  • Containing the area so contamination does not spread to clean rooms
  • Protective gear for the crew: gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators
  • Pumping and extracting sewage and solids
  • Removing and bagging contaminated carpet, pad, drywall and insulation
  • Cleaning hard surfaces, then applying disinfectant
  • Drying the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers
  • Odor control once the source and materials are gone
  • Photos and records for your insurance claim

Three categories of water

The restoration industry sorts water by how contaminated it is. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard defines three categories, and sewage is always the worst.

The sewage cleanup process

  1. Stop using water. Don't flush, run showers or start the washer until a plumber clears the line.
  2. Contain and protect. The crew seals off the area with plastic sheeting and suits up.
  3. Remove sewage and solids. Pumps and extractors remove liquid; solids are shoveled and bagged.
  4. Tear-out. Porous materials that sewage touched, including carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are removed and disposed of.
  5. Clean and disinfect. Hard surfaces are scrubbed, then treated with disinfectant. Products are never mixed; EPA guidance warns not to add bleach to other chemicals.
  6. Dry and verify. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, then the crew confirms it is clean and dry before repairs.

Health and safety

Sewage carries bacteria, viruses and parasites. EPA flood guidance warns that floodwater can carry sewage and that wet materials become a breeding ground for germs and mold. Keep children, pets, pregnant people and anyone with asthma or a weakened immune system away from the area. Don't track contaminated water into clean rooms, and wash your hands well after any contact. If you get sick after exposure, see a doctor.

How to prevent sewage backups

  • Never flush wipes, even ones labeled "flushable," or pour grease down drains.
  • Have the sewer line camera-inspected if you have large trees or an older home.
  • Ask a plumber about a backwater (backflow) valve on the main line.
  • Keep a basement floor drain plug or standpipe if your area floods.
  • Fix slow drains early, before a full blockage.

What affects the cost of sewage cleanup

  • Area affected and whether sewage reached finished rooms.
  • Depth and solids that must be removed and disposed of.
  • Tear-out of carpet, drywall, insulation and cabinets.
  • Disinfection and drying time.
  • Plumbing repairs to clear or replace the line, billed separately.

Insurance notes

Sewer and drain backup is often excluded from a standard homeowners policy unless you added a water backup endorsement. A sewage backup caused by outside flooding may fall under flood insurance instead. If the blockage was in a city main, your city may have a claims process. Policies differ, so read yours, photograph the damage and report the loss quickly.
