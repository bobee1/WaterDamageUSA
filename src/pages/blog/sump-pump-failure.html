Short answer: Sump pump failure is most often caused by a power outage, a stuck float switch, a clogged or frozen discharge line, an undersized pump, or simple age. If your basement floods, cut power to the basement if it's safe, remove the water, and dry everything within 24 to 48 hours. A battery backup and a yearly test prevent most failures.

A sump pump sits quietly in the basement until the one night you need it. When it fails during a storm, groundwater can cover the floor in hours. If that's happened, DryFlare can help with basement flood cleanup and drying. This guide explains why pumps fail and how to keep yours working.

Why do sump pumps fail?

Most failures come down to power, the switch or the discharge line. Here are the common causes and what to check.

CauseWhat happensWhat helps
Power outageThe pump stops during the storm that knocked out powerBattery backup or water-powered backup pump
Stuck float switchThe pump never turns on, or never turns offKeep the pit clean; test the float each season
Clogged or frozen discharge lineWater can't leave, so it cycles back into the pitClear the outlet; add a freeze guard
Undersized pumpIt runs nonstop and still falls behindHave a plumber size the pump to the inflow
Debris in the pitGravel or silt jams the intake or floatClean the pit and use a lid
Age and wearThe motor burns out after years of cyclingReplace on schedule, before it fails
Missing check valveWater flows back into the pit after each cycleInstall or replace the check valve

Warning signs of a failing sump pump

  • The pump runs all the time, even in dry weather
  • It cycles on and off every few seconds
  • Strange grinding, rattling or humming noises
  • The pump vibrates hard when it runs
  • Visible rust or a damp, musty pit
  • Water stays in the pit above the float level

Any of these means it's time for a closer look before the next heavy rain.

What to do if your basement floods

  1. Stay safe. Don't walk into water that may touch outlets, cords or appliances. Shut off basement power at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground.
  2. Find out why. Check whether the pump has power, the float is stuck, or the discharge line is blocked.
  3. Get the water out. Use a working pump or a wet-dry vacuum for shallow water. Deep water needs a trash pump or a pro.
  4. Document the damage. Take photos and video before you move or throw anything away.
  5. Remove wet items. Move boxes, rugs and furniture out. The CDC says to toss drywall and insulation soaked by floodwater.
  6. Dry within 24 to 48 hours. The EPA says quick drying is the best way to prevent mold.

If the water smells like sewage or came up through a floor drain, treat it as contaminated. Our sewage backup guide explains the extra safety steps.

How long do sump pumps last?

There's no single answer. Lifespan depends on the pump's build and how hard it works. A pump in a high water table may run many times a day and wear out years sooner than one that rarely turns on. Check the manufacturer's rated life and warranty, write the install date on the pit lid, and replace the pump near the end of that rating. Grinding noises or nonstop running mean it may be failing early.

How to test a sump pump

  1. Check the power. Make sure it's plugged into a working GFCI outlet.
  2. Pour water into the pit slowly until the float rises.
  3. Watch it run. The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off.
  4. Check outside. Water should exit the discharge line well away from the house.
  5. Test the backup by unplugging the main pump and repeating the test.

Test every spring and fall, and before big storms.

Does insurance cover sump pump failure?

Often not, unless you added water backup coverage. The NAIC lists water backup as an optional coverage that pays for damage from sewer or drain backup. Many insurers include sump pump overflow in that add-on. Flood from rising water outside needs a separate flood policy through FEMA's program or a private insurer. See does homeowners insurance cover water damage for the full picture.

How to prevent sump pump failure

  • Add a backup: battery or water-powered, so outages don't stop it.
  • Install a high-water alarm that sounds or texts you if the pit fills.
  • Keep the pit clean and covered.
  • Extend the discharge line at least several feet from the foundation.
  • Replace the pump on schedule instead of waiting for it to quit.
  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so less water reaches the pit.

When should you call a flood cleanup professional?

  • More than an inch or two of water covers the floor
  • A finished basement, drywall or carpet got wet
  • The water smells like sewage or came through a drain
  • Water sat for more than a day
  • The furnace, water heater or electrical panel got wet
