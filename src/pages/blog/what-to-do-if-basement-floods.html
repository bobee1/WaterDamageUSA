Short answer: If your basement floods, stay out of the water until the power is off. Then stop the source if you can, take photos, remove the water with a pump or wet vac, and move wet items out. Dry everything within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold, and call your insurer. Floodwater or sewage needs extra care and usually a professional.

Walking down the stairs into standing water is a sinking feeling. What you do in the first few hours decides how much you can save. This guide walks through each step in order. If the water is deep, dirty or spreading, DryFlare can start basement flood cleanup right away.

Step 1: Make sure it is safe

Water and electricity are a deadly mix. Before anyone steps into the basement:

  • If water is near outlets, cords, appliances or the panel, do not go in. Call your utility or an electrician to cut power.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas company from outside.
  • If the water may contain sewage, wear rubber boots, gloves and eye protection, as the CDC advises, and keep children and pets away.
  • Watch for weak stairs, bulging walls or a sagging ceiling.

Step 2: Find and stop the source

Knowing where the water came from tells you what to do next and how insurance will treat it.

SourceCluesFirst move
Burst pipe or water heaterClean water, steady flow, wet near plumbingShut off the main water valve
Sump pump failureWater around the pit, often in heavy rain or a power outageRestore power or start a backup pump
Sewer or floor drain backupDirty water and odor coming up through a drainStop using water upstairs; call a pro
Groundwater or surface floodingWater through walls, windows or the floor after stormsWait for water outside to fall before pumping

Pumping too fast after a big flood can be risky. When the ground outside is saturated, emptying the basement all at once can let outside water push on the walls. If the yard is still flooded, pump slowly or wait for a pro.

Step 3: Document everything

Take photos and video of the water level, damaged items, walls and floors before you move things. Note the date and time. Keep receipts for anything you buy. Your insurer will want this, and it is hard to recreate later.

Step 4: Remove the water

For an inch or two of clean water, a wet vac works. For more, use a submersible pump with a hose run well away from the house. Professionals use truck-mounted and portable extractors that pull water out of carpet and pad too. See our water extraction service for what that involves.

Step 5: Move out wet items and open the space

Take furniture, boxes, rugs and stored items out to dry or to be thrown away. Pull up wet carpet and pad. The CDC says drywall and insulation soaked by floodwater or sewage should be removed. Cutting drywall a foot or two above the wet line lets the wall cavity dry.

Step 6: Dry it within 24 to 48 hours

The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. That means running fans and dehumidifiers day and night, and keeping the space closed so the dehumidifiers pull water out of the air instead of the whole outdoors. Learn how in our guide to dehumidifiers for water damage.

Concrete floors and block walls hold water for a long time. They can feel dry while still wet inside. Moisture meters show when the space is truly dry, which is why structural drying is often worth it for a finished basement.

Step 7: Clean and check for mold

Clean hard surfaces with soap and water once they are dry enough to work on. After floodwater or sewage, disinfect too. If you see or smell mold, the EPA says areas under about 10 square feet can usually be cleaned at home. Larger areas need professional mold remediation. See mold after water damage for more.

Does insurance cover a flooded basement?

It depends on where the water came from:

  • Burst pipe or appliance leak: usually covered as sudden water damage.
  • Sump pump failure or sewer backup: usually needs a water backup endorsement, which the NAIC lists as an optional add-on.
  • Water from outside (rain, rivers, groundwater): needs flood insurance through FEMA's program or a private insurer.

How to keep your basement from flooding again

  • Test the sump pump each spring and add a battery backup (see sump pump failure).
  • Install a backwater valve if water has come up through a floor drain.
  • Keep gutters clean and extend downspouts several feet from the house.
  • Grade soil so it slopes away from the foundation.
  • Store valuables on shelves, not on the floor.
  • Add water backup and flood coverage before you need it.

Local note: Basement floods are a spring ritual in much of the Midwest and Northeast. Snowmelt and storms fill basements in Illinois, Michigan, Ohio and Pennsylvania, while combined sewers back up in older cities like Detroit and Chicago.

When should you call a professional?

  • More than an inch or two of water covers the floor
  • The water came from a drain, a sewer line or outside
  • A finished basement, drywall or carpet got wet
  • The furnace, water heater or electrical panel got wet
  • Water sat for more than a day, or you smell mold
