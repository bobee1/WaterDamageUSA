Short answer: The most common signs of water damage in walls are stains, bubbling or peeling paint, soft or swollen drywall, warped baseboards, a musty smell and mold spots. A jump in your water bill can point to a hidden plumbing leak. Find and fix the source, then dry the wall within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

Water inside a wall is easy to miss. It can drip from a pipe for weeks, soaking insulation and framing, before anything shows on the surface. By the time you see a stain, the damage behind it is often larger. This guide covers the clues to look for and what to do. If you find a wet wall, DryFlare offers water damage repair and drying.

9 signs of water damage in walls

  1. Stains or discoloration. Yellow, brown or gray patches, often with a darker ring at the edge.
  2. Bubbling, peeling or cracking paint. Water pushes paint away from the drywall.
  3. Soft or swollen drywall. Press gently; wet drywall feels spongy or crumbles.
  4. Warped or stained baseboards. Water runs down inside the wall and pools at the bottom.
  5. A musty smell. Often the first sign, especially in closets and bathrooms.
  6. Mold spots. Black, green or gray specks near the floor, ceiling or behind furniture.
  7. Cupped or lifting floors near the wall. Wood and laminate swell when water reaches them.
  8. A rising water bill. With no change in use, it can mean a hidden leak.
  9. The sound of dripping or running water when every tap is off.

How to confirm a leak behind the wall

  • Check your water meter. Turn off every faucet and appliance, note the meter, and check it again in an hour. If it moved, water is leaking somewhere.
  • Use a moisture meter. These are cheap at hardware stores and show how wet drywall is compared with a dry wall nearby.
  • Look for a pattern. Damage near a bathroom, kitchen or laundry on the other side of the wall points to plumbing. Damage near windows or the ceiling after rain points to the roof or flashing.
  • Call a pro for thermal imaging. A thermal camera shows cool, damp spots inside a wall without cutting it open.

What to do when you find water damage in a wall

  1. Stop the water. If it is plumbing, shut off the fixture valve or the main. If it is the roof, get a tarp or roofer.
  2. Turn off power to outlets on the wet wall if you can do it safely.
  3. Take photos for your insurer before anything is opened up.
  4. Open the wall if needed. Removing baseboards or cutting an inspection hole lets air reach the cavity. In homes built before 1978, the EPA warns that paint may contain lead, so follow lead-safe practices or hire a contractor trained in lead-safe work.
  5. Dry it fast. The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. Air movers and dehumidifiers, and sometimes special wall-drying systems, do the job; this is part of structural drying.
  6. Repair once it is dry, confirmed with a moisture meter, not by touch.

When wet drywall can be saved

Drywall wet with clean water can sometimes be dried in place if it is caught within a day or two and stays firm. It should be replaced if it is soft, sagging or crumbling, shows mold, or was soaked by floodwater or sewage. Insulation behind it usually needs to come out when it is soaked. See mold after water damage for what to do if mold has started.

Does insurance cover water damage in walls?

It depends on how it happened. The NAIC says sudden water damage, like a pipe that bursts inside a wall, is generally covered. Damage from a slow leak that went on for a long time is often treated as wear and tear and denied. Report damage as soon as you find it, and keep records of when you noticed it and what you did. See does homeowners insurance cover water damage.

Local note: Hidden wall leaks take different forms by region. Copper pinhole leaks are common in parts of Arizona, aging galvanized pipe in older Colorado and Midwest homes, and roof and window leaks during storms in Florida and Texas.

When should you call a professional?

  • The wall is soft, sagging or stained over a large area
  • You can't find the source of the leak
  • You smell mold or see mold spots
  • Water reached outlets or wiring
  • The wall stayed wet for more than a day or two
