Short answer: Mold can start growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours after water damage. The best defense is drying everything fast. Small areas of mold, under about 10 square feet, can usually be cleaned at home. Larger areas, mold inside walls, or mold after sewage or floodwater need a professional. Insurance may cover mold from a sudden, covered leak, often with a limit.

Mold is the reason water damage can't wait. A leak that is dried in a day or two is usually just a repair. The same leak left for a week can turn into a mold problem that costs far more. This guide explains how mold grows, what to look for and what to do. For larger growth, see our mold remediation service.

How fast does mold grow after water damage?

Mold spores are in almost every home, floating in the air and settling on surfaces. They stay dormant until they find moisture and something to feed on, like drywall paper, wood, carpet backing or dust. The EPA says to dry wet building materials and furnishings within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

Several things speed it up:

  • Warmth: most household molds grow fastest at normal room temperatures.
  • Humidity: the EPA advises keeping indoor humidity below 60%, and ideally between 30% and 50%.
  • Poor airflow: closets, wall cavities and crawl spaces stay damp longer.
  • Dirty water: gray and black water carry nutrients and germs that make things worse.

Signs of mold after a leak or flood

  • A musty, earthy smell, especially in a closed room or basement
  • Black, green, gray or white spots on walls, ceilings, baseboards or furniture
  • Peeling, bubbling or stained paint near where water was
  • Warped wood or soft drywall that stayed wet
  • Allergy-like symptoms that get better when you leave the house

Mold often hides. A clean-looking wall can have growth on the back of the drywall. If you had a leak and now smell mustiness, the source is likely behind a surface. Our guide to signs of water damage in walls explains how to check.

Can you clean mold yourself?

For small, surface mold on hard materials, often yes. The EPA says you can usually clean an area under about 10 square feet yourself. Follow these steps:

  1. Fix the water problem first. Mold comes back if the moisture stays.
  2. Protect yourself. Wear gloves, goggles and an N95 respirator, as the EPA and CDC advise.
  3. Scrub hard surfaces with detergent and water, then dry them completely.
  4. Throw out porous items with mold, like ceiling tiles, carpet and drywall that can't be cleaned.
  5. Do not just paint over it. Paint over mold peels and the mold keeps growing.

How to prevent mold after water damage

Prevention is mostly about speed and dryness:

  • Stop the leak and remove standing water right away (see water extraction).
  • Pull up wet carpet pad and cut out soaked drywall and insulation.
  • Run air movers and dehumidifiers until materials are dry, not just dry to the touch. Our guide to dehumidifiers for water damage explains how.
  • Keep indoor humidity below 60%.
  • Have a pro check with moisture meters if water got into walls or floors.

This is the core of water mitigation: drying the structure fast enough that mold never gets started.

Does insurance cover mold from water damage?

It depends on what caused the moisture. Mold that grows after a sudden, covered event, like a burst pipe that you dealt with quickly, may be covered. Many policies cap mold coverage at a set amount, and some exclude it unless you buy an add-on. Mold from slow leaks, long-term humidity or poor maintenance is usually excluded. The NAIC suggests reading your policy and asking your agent about mold limits. See does homeowners insurance cover water damage for more.

Local note: Mold moves fastest in hot, humid places. After leaks and storms in Florida, Louisiana and Georgia, wet drywall can grow mold within a day or two. Damp crawl spaces in North Carolina and the Pacific Northwest are another common source.

When should you call a professional?

  • You see more than a small patch of mold
  • You smell mustiness but can't find the source
  • Mold followed sewage, floodwater or a long leak
  • Mold came back after cleaning
  • Someone in the home has breathing problems
