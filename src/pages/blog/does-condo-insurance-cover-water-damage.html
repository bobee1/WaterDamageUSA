Short answer: Usually yes, for sudden water damage like a burst pipe or overflowing appliance. But in a condo, two policies share the cost. The association's master policy covers the building and common areas. Your HO-6 policy covers your belongings, the parts of the unit you own and your liability. Who pays depends on where the leak started and your association's rules. Flood needs a separate policy.

Water damage in a condo is rarely just one owner's problem. A leak in one unit can travel through floors and walls into two or three others, and every owner may have a different idea of who should pay. This guide explains how condo coverage works. Whoever pays, the first job is to stop the water and start drying; DryFlare handles ceiling and wall leaks in condos and apartments.

How condo insurance works: two policies

PolicyWho buys itWhat it usually covers
Master policyThe condo associationThe building structure, roof, common areas and shared pipes; sometimes interior finishes
HO-6 (unit owner) policyEach ownerYour belongings, your part of the unit, liability, loss assessment and extra living costs

The line between the two is set by your association's governing documents, often called the declaration or bylaws. Read them, or ask your manager for a summary.

Bare walls vs. all-in master policies

Master policies come in a few types, and the type decides how much your HO-6 needs to cover:

  • Bare walls: covers the structure only. You insure everything inside, including drywall, floors, cabinets, fixtures and appliances.
  • Single entity: covers the structure plus original fixtures and finishes, but not upgrades you added.
  • All-in: covers the structure, fixtures and most improvements inside the unit.

If you remodeled your kitchen or added hardwood floors, make sure your HO-6 limit covers those upgrades.

Who pays for common condo water leaks?

CauseWho often pays
Pipe inside a shared wall or the main stack failsAssociation's master policy for the building parts; your HO-6 for your belongings
Upstairs owner's washer, tub or water heater overflowsUpstairs owner's HO-6 liability, or the master policy depending on the bylaws
Your own supply line or toilet failsYour HO-6 for your unit; your liability coverage for damage below
Roof leakAssociation's master policy
Flood from outsideFlood insurance only

These are common patterns, not rules. Bylaws vary, and some associations make the owner where the leak started responsible for the master policy deductible.

What is loss assessment coverage?

Master policies often carry large deductibles. When a loss is under that deductible or above the policy limit, the association may charge owners an assessment to cover the gap. Loss assessment coverage on your HO-6 helps pay your share. It is often included or offered as an add-on, and many owners raise the limit to match the master deductible.

What condo insurance usually does not cover

  • Flood: rising water from outside needs flood insurance. FloodSmart explains that NFIP coverage is available to condo owners and associations.
  • Slow leaks and wear: damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months is often denied as a maintenance issue.
  • Mold from long-term moisture: often excluded or capped. See mold after water damage.
  • Sewer backup: often needs a water backup add-on.

What to do when a condo leak happens

  1. Stop the water. Shut off your unit's valve, or alert the building manager to shut off a riser.
  2. Tell everyone affected: the manager or board, the owners above and below, and your insurer.
  3. Document it. Take photos and video, and keep notes on who you talked to and when.
  4. Start drying fast. The EPA says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Waiting for insurers to agree on who pays can turn a small claim into a big one. Professional water mitigation limits the damage while the claim is sorted out.
  5. Keep receipts for repairs, drying and any hotel stay.

When should you call a professional?

  • Water came through a ceiling or wall from another unit
  • More than one unit is affected
  • Drywall, flooring or cabinets are wet
  • The leak ran for more than a few hours
  • You smell mold or see stains spreading
