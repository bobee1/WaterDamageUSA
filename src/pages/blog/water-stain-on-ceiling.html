Short answer: A water stain on the ceiling means water reached the drywall from above: a roof leak, a plumbing leak, an AC drain, or condensation. Fix the source first, confirm the drywall is dry, then seal the stain with a stain-blocking primer and repaint. Sagging or soft drywall needs to be cut out and replaced.

A brown ring on the ceiling is easy to ignore, but it's a message. Water got into the ceiling, and it may still be getting in. Small, dry, old stains are a paint job. Wet, growing or sagging stains need attention fast. DryBeacon handles ceiling water damage repair, from drying to new drywall and paint.

What causes a water stain on the ceiling?

The cause usually depends on where the stain is. Look at what sits above it.

Location of the stainLikely causeWho to call
Top floor, after rainRoof leak, flashing, vents or skylightRoofer
Top floor, in winterIce dam or attic condensationRoofer or insulation pro
Under a bathroomToilet seal, shower pan, tub drain or supply linePlumber

Water travels along joists and pipes before it shows up, so the stain may be a few feet from the actual leak. A water heater in the attic or upstairs can also be the source; see our guide to a water heater leaking from the bottom.

How to tell if the ceiling is still wet

Press gently near the stain. Soft or spongy drywall is wet. Other signs:

  • The stain gets bigger or darker after rain or a shower
  • Bubbling or peeling paint
  • A sag or bulge in the drywall
  • Drips, or a musty smell
  • A moisture meter reads higher than the dry ceiling nearby

A sagging ceiling can hold water. Keep people out from under it, put a bucket below, and turn off lights and fans in that ceiling.

How to fix a water stain on the ceiling

  1. Stop the leak. Have the roof, plumbing or AC repaired. Painting first just hides the problem.
  2. Dry the area. Ceilings can take days to dry. Air movers and a dehumidifier speed it up. Insulation above may need to be removed.
  3. Check for mold. The EPA says you can clean small areas yourself, under about 10 square feet. Bigger areas should be handled by a pro.
  4. Repair damaged drywall. Cut out soft or sagging sections and patch them.
  5. Seal the stain. Use a stain-blocking primer made for water stains.
  6. Repaint. Paint the whole ceiling plane, not just the spot, so the color matches.

How to cover water stains on a ceiling

Use a stain-blocking primer, then ceiling paint. Water stains carry minerals and tannins from wood, which bleed through regular paint, sometimes within days. Oil-based or shellac-based stain blockers seal best. Let the primer dry fully, then apply one or two coats of flat ceiling paint.

Older homes need care. The EPA says homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint. Sanding or scraping old ceiling paint can create lead dust. Mist the area first, contain the dust, or hire an EPA Lead-Safe firm.

When a ceiling stain needs more than paint

Repair, not just paint, is needed when:

  • The drywall is soft, crumbling or sagging
  • The stain is larger than about two square feet and keeps coming back
  • Insulation above the ceiling is wet
  • You see mold spots or smell mold
  • The ceiling is plaster with cracks or loose sections

Drying matters as much as repair. Our guide to dehumidifiers for water damage explains why closed-up ceiling spaces dry slowly.

Does insurance cover ceiling water damage?

Often, if the leak was sudden, like a burst pipe or a storm-damaged roof. The NAIC says homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental discharges from plumbing. A slow leak that stained the ceiling over months may be treated as maintenance. In a condo or rental, the building's policy and yours split the cost; see renters insurance and water damage.

How to prevent ceiling water stains

  • Clean gutters each fall and check roof flashing after storms.
  • Flush the AC condensate line each spring and check the overflow pan.
  • Re-caulk tubs and showers when the caulk cracks.
  • Replace toilet supply lines and wax rings when they show wear.
  • Insulate and ventilate the attic to prevent ice dams.

Local note: Ice dams stain ceilings every winter in Boston, across Massachusetts and Maine. Flat roofs and clogged canales cause stains in New Mexico, and attic AC leaks are common in Florida.

When should you call a ceiling water damage professional?

  • The ceiling is sagging, dripping or soft
  • The stain keeps growing
  • Insulation or framing above is wet
  • There is visible mold or a musty smell
  • You can't find the source of the leak
