Short answer: A sewage backup in the basement is usually caused by a clog in your sewer line, tree roots, or a city sewer overloaded by heavy rain. Stop using water, keep everyone away, and wear protective gear. Sewage is Category 3 black water, so porous items it soaked must be thrown out and hard surfaces cleaned and disinfected.

Sewage in the basement is one of the worst things a homeowner can find. It smells, it's a health risk, and it spreads fast. DryBeacon handles sewage cleanup with protective gear, removal and disinfection. This guide covers what to do first and how to stop it from happening again.

This is general information, not medical advice. If anyone gets sick after contact with sewage, call a doctor.

What causes sewage backup in a basement?

Backups happen when waste can't get out of the house fast enough. The cause decides who fixes it.

CauseSignsWho usually fixes it
Clog in your sewer lineSlow drains, gurgling, backup when you run waterA plumber or drain cleaner
Tree roots in the lineRepeated clogs, worse over timeA plumber; the line may need repair
Grease, wipes and debrisKitchen or toilet drains back upA plumber; change what goes down the drain
City sewer overload in heavy rainBackup during or right after stormsThe utility; you can add a backwater valve
Collapsed or broken lineBackups, sinkholes or soggy yardA plumber; excavation or lining

Older cities with combined sewers carry both rainwater and sewage in the same pipes. In big storms they can overload and push sewage into basements through floor drains.

What to do first when sewage backs up

  1. Stop using water. Don't flush, shower or run the washer. Every gallon goes back to the basement.
  2. Keep people and pets away. Especially children and anyone with health problems.
  3. Shut off power to the basement if you can reach the panel safely from a dry spot.
  4. Put on protective gear. The CDC recommends goggles, an N95 respirator and gloves, plus rubber boots.
  5. Open windows for air if you can do it without walking through sewage.
  6. Call a plumber and the city. Find out whether the blockage is in your line or the main.
  7. Document it. Take photos and video for your insurer before cleanup.

How to clean up a sewage backup

Small, shallow backups on bare concrete can be handled carefully at home. Anything larger is a job for a pro. If you clean it yourself:

  • Remove the sewage with a wet-dry vacuum or pump, and bag solids for the trash.
  • Throw out porous items it touched. The CDC says not to keep items that can't be cleaned with bleach, such as mattresses, carpet and carpet padding.
  • Cut out wet drywall and insulation. The CDC says to discard drywall and insulation soaked by sewage.
  • Wash hard surfaces with soap and water, then disinfect. The CDC suggests 1 cup of household bleach in 1 gallon of water for cleaning.
  • Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners; the fumes are toxic.
  • Dry everything within 24 to 48 hours with fans and dehumidifiers.
  • Wash up. Wash hands well and launder work clothes separately in hot water.

Who is responsible for a sewage backup?

It depends on where the problem is. In many cities, the homeowner owns the sewer lateral, the pipe from the house to the city main. A clog or root problem there is usually yours to fix. A blocked or overloaded city main is the utility's problem, though they may not pay for damage inside your home. Check with your local sewer utility for their rules.

Does insurance cover sewage backup?

Only if you have water backup coverage. The NAIC lists water backup of sewer as an optional coverage that pays for damage from sewer or drain backup. Without it, a standard policy usually won't pay. If the backup happened because of a general flood, flood insurance may apply instead. See does homeowners insurance cover water damage, and if you rent, read renters insurance and water damage.

How to prevent sewage backup in the basement

  • Install a backwater valve. It lets waste flow out but closes if sewage tries to flow back in. A plumber installs it, and many cities require a permit.
  • Don't flush wipes or pour grease down drains.
  • Have the line scoped with a camera if you have repeat clogs or big trees nearby.
  • Disconnect downspouts and sump pumps from the sewer if your city requires it.
  • Add water backup coverage to your home policy.
  • Use a standpipe or drain plug on floor drains during big storms if you don't have a valve.

Groundwater flooding is a different problem with a different fix; see sump pump failure.

Local note: Combined sewers overload in heavy rain in older cities, including Chicago, Detroit, Milwaukee and Washington, DC. Many of these cities have programs that help pay for backwater valves.

When should you call a sewage cleanup professional?

  • Sewage covers more than a small patch of bare concrete
  • It reached carpet, drywall or finished rooms
  • The backup keeps coming back
  • Anyone in the home is very young, elderly or has a weak immune system
  • You need documentation for an insurance claim
