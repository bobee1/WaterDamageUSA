Short answer: Water damage restoration cost depends on how much area got wet, what kind of water it was, how long it sat, what materials must be torn out, and how many days of drying it takes. A clean leak caught early costs far less than sewage or floodwater left for days. The best way to know your cost is a written, itemized estimate after an inspection.

When water hits your home, cost is one of the first worries. Every job is different, so we don't publish price lists that may not fit your home. What decides water damage restoration cost?

Seven factors drive most of the price. The first two matter most.

FactorWhy it changes the price
Square footage affectedMore wet area means more extraction, equipment and labor
Water categoryClean (Category 1), gray (2) or black water (3); dirty water adds protective gear, removal and disinfection
Damage classHow much of the room got wet and how deep water soaked in sets the drying equipment needed
Time before drying startedWater that sits spreads, grows mold and can turn clean water into contaminated water
MaterialsHardwood, plaster and finished basements cost more to dry or replace than tile or bare concrete
Tear-out and repairsRemoving and rebuilding drywall, flooring and cabinets is often the biggest line
Access and locationCrawl spaces, attics, high-rises and local labor rates all matter

Water category: clean, gray and black water

The industry standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, sorts water into three categories. The category is often the biggest cost jump.

  • Category 1 (clean): a broken supply line or a sink overflow. Many materials can be dried in place.
  • Category 2 (gray): washer or dishwasher discharge. Carpet pad often has to go.
  • Category 3 (black): sewage or floodwater. Porous materials it touched usually must be removed, and the area cleaned and disinfected. The CDC says to discard drywall and insulation soaked by sewage or floodwater.

Clean water doesn't stay clean. As it sits, it picks up dirt and bacteria. That's one reason fast action saves money.

How a water damage estimate is built

A good estimate lists each task, not one lump sum. Expect lines like these:

  1. Emergency service and inspection. Finding the source, moisture mapping and photos.
  2. Water extraction. Pumping and extracting standing water from floors and carpet.
  3. Tear-out. Removing wet drywall, insulation, baseboards, carpet pad or flooring.
  4. Drying equipment. Air movers and dehumidifiers, usually priced per unit per day.
  5. Monitoring. Daily moisture readings until materials reach the dry standard.
  6. Cleaning and antimicrobial treatment, especially for gray or black water.
  7. Repairs. New drywall, paint, flooring, trim and cabinets.

Many insurers and restoration companies use Xactimate, a program that prices each line with regional rates. That makes it easier to compare a contractor's estimate with an adjuster's.

Why drying time affects the price

Drying equipment is often billed by the day, so faster drying costs less. Drying usually takes three to five days for a typical room, longer for hardwood, plaster or concrete. Under-drying to save a day is a false saving: the EPA warns that mold can grow if materials stay wet beyond 24 to 48 hours. Does insurance cover water damage restoration?

Often, when the cause is sudden and accidental. The NAIC says homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental discharges from plumbing. You pay the deductible, and the insurer pays the covered cost. Flood is separate; FloodSmart says NFIP building policies cover up to $250,000. Read what homeowners insurance covers and, for tenants, renters insurance and water damage.

How to keep water damage costs down

  • Act in the first hours. Shut off the water and call for help right away.
  • Move belongings out of wet areas so fewer contents are ruined.
  • Take photos before cleanup to support your claim.
  • Ask for an itemized estimate and moisture readings at the start and end.
  • Don't skip drying to save money; hidden moisture costs more later.
  • Prevent repeats with leak sensors and an automatic shut-off valve.

Local note: Labor and material costs vary by region. Large hurricane events in Florida and Louisiana strain crews and supplies. Finished basements in Colorado and Minnesota add drywall and flooring to many jobs.

When should you call a water damage professional?

  • Water reached drywall, carpet or flooring in more than one spot

  • The damage sat for more than a day
