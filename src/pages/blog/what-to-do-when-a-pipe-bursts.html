Short answer: When a pipe bursts, shut off the main water valve right away, turn off power to wet areas, and open faucets to drain the lines. Then remove standing water, take photos for insurance, and start drying within 24 to 48 hours. Call a plumber to fix the pipe and a restoration company if water has spread.

A burst pipe can release a lot of water in minutes, and the first few minutes matter most. Knowing where your shut-off valve is turns a disaster into a cleanup. What to do when a pipe bursts: the first 10 minutes

  1. Shut off the main water. The main valve is usually where the water line enters the house: in a basement, crawl space, garage, utility closet or near the water meter.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas. Use the breaker panel only if you can reach it without standing in water.
  3. Open faucets. Open cold taps, and the lowest faucet in the house, to drain the lines and relieve pressure.
  4. Shut off the water heater if hot water lines burst, by turning off its power or gas.
  5. Move valuables. Get electronics, papers and rugs out of the wet area.
  6. Catch and remove water. Use buckets, towels and a wet-dry vacuum.

Teach everyone in the house where the main shut-off is. Tag it so it's easy to find in a hurry.

How can you tell if a pipe burst?

Not every burst is obvious. Pipes inside walls, under floors or in crawl spaces can leak out of sight. Watch for these signs:

  • A sudden drop in water pressure at faucets
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on
  • Water stains, bubbling paint or wet spots on walls and ceilings
  • A water meter that keeps moving with all taps off
  • A water bill that jumps for no reason
  • Puddles, musty smells or warped flooring
  • No water at all from a faucet in cold weather, which can mean a frozen line

A stain on the ceiling below a bathroom is a common first sign; see water stain on the ceiling for what to check.

Why do pipes burst?

Freezing causes most winter bursts. When water freezes, pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet, and the pipe splits. That's why the split is often not where the ice is. Other causes:

Do frozen pipes always burst?

No, but it's a real risk. If a faucet only drips or nothing comes out in freezing weather, a pipe may be frozen. The Red Cross advises keeping the faucet open while you thaw the pipe, and applying heat with a hair dryer, heating pad or space heater. Never use an open flame. If you can't find or reach the frozen spot, call a plumber.

After the water is off: cleanup and drying

Once the pipe is fixed, the drying starts. Water from a supply line is clean at first, but it soaks into drywall, insulation and subfloors fast. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Wet insulation and soaked drywall often need to come out so the wall cavity can dry. Learn how drying works in dehumidifiers for water damage.

Does insurance cover a burst pipe?

Usually, yes, for the water damage. The NAIC says homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental discharges from plumbing. But policies generally require you to keep the home at a minimum temperature, or shut off the water if it's vacant. A pipe that froze in an unheated, empty house may be denied. How to keep pipes from bursting

  • Insulate exposed pipes in attics, crawl spaces, garages and outside walls.
  • Disconnect hoses and shut off outside faucets before winter.
  • Keep the heat on. The Red Cross suggests keeping the thermostat at the same temperature day and night during cold snaps.
  • Let faucets drip on very cold nights, and open cabinet doors so warm air reaches the pipes.
  • Control water pressure with a pressure reducing valve.
  • Add leak sensors and an automatic shut-off valve, especially in second homes.

When should you call a water damage professional?

  • Water reached walls, ceilings, carpet or wood floors
  • The pipe burst inside a wall or ceiling
  • More than one room is wet
  • Water ran for a while before you found it
  • You need moisture readings and photos for your insurer
