Burst Pipe Water Damage Cleanup

A burst pipe can release a lot of water in a short time, often inside walls and ceilings. Shut off the main valve, then call DryBeacon to get the water out and the structure drying.

Quick answer: When a pipe bursts, shut off the main water valve, cut power to wet areas from a dry spot and call for help. A plumber fixes the pipe; a water damage crew removes the water, opens up wet walls and dries the structure. Burst-pipe water is usually clean at first, but it spreads into walls and floors quickly, so drying should start the same day.

What to do right now

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually where the water line enters the home, in a basement, crawl space, utility closet or near the water heater. In a condo, it may be inside the unit near the water heater or kitchen.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas from the breaker panel, only if you can reach it from a dry spot.
  3. Open faucets to drain the remaining water out of the lines.
  4. Move what you can. Pull rugs, electronics, papers and furniture out of the water.
  5. Photograph everything before cleanup: the pipe, the water and damaged items.
  6. Call a plumber and a restoration crew. In a condo or apartment, also tell the building manager and the units below you.

Why pipes burst

Water expands as it freezes, which puts pressure on pipes from the inside. That is why a pipe often leaks only once it thaws. The Red Cross lists exterior walls and the point where water enters through the foundation as likely places for frozen pipes.

How burst pipe water damage is cleaned up

  1. Plumbing repair. A licensed plumber fixes the pipe so the water can be turned back on.
  2. Extraction. Standing water is removed from floors and carpet. See water extraction.
  3. Finding hidden water. Thermal cameras and moisture meters show how far water traveled in walls and ceilings.
  4. Controlled tear-out. Small cuts in drywall or ceilings let trapped water out and air in, often saving the rest of the wall.
  5. Drying. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, with readings each visit.
  6. Repairs. New drywall, paint and flooring once the structure is dry.

Burst pipes in ceilings and upper floors

When a pipe bursts on an upper floor or in an attic, water runs down through the ceiling below. Ceiling drywall soaked with water can sag and fall, and insulation above it holds water for days. If a ceiling is bulging, keep people out of the room and do not poke it while standing underneath. Read more about ceiling water damage repair.

How to prevent frozen pipes

The American Red Cross recommends these steps before and during cold weather:

  • Drain water from swimming pool and sprinkler supply lines, and drain and store outdoor hoses.
  • Close inside valves to outdoor hose bibs and open the outside bibs to let them drain.
  • Add insulation to attics, basements and crawl spaces.
  • Keep garage doors closed if water lines run through the garage.
  • Open kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors so warm air reaches the plumbing.
  • Let cold water drip from faucets served by exposed pipes.
  • Keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night, and no lower than 55°F if you go away.

To thaw a frozen pipe, the Red Cross advises a hair dryer, space heater or warm towels, and never an open flame. If you cannot find or reach the frozen section, call a licensed plumber.

What affects the cost of burst pipe cleanup

  • How long the pipe ran before it was shut off, and how much water was released.
  • Where the water went: into walls, ceilings, a finished basement or the unit below.
  • Materials affected, such as hardwood, cabinets or plaster.
  • Drying time and how much equipment is needed.
  • Repairs after drying, plus the separate plumbing bill.

Insurance notes

A sudden burst pipe is usually a covered water loss under a homeowners policy, while the repair of the pipe itself may not be. Some policies exclude freeze damage when a home was left without heat or not checked during cold weather. Condo owners should check both their own policy and the association's master policy. Report the loss right away and keep the plumber's invoice, which shows the cause.

Burst pipe water damage questions

What should I do first when a pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water valve, usually where the water line enters the house or near the water heater. Then turn off power to wet areas from a dry spot, open faucets to drain the lines, move valuables out of the water and call a plumber for the pipe and a restoration crew for the water.

Who fixes the pipe: a plumber or a restoration company?

A licensed plumber repairs or replaces the broken pipe. The restoration crew handles the water damage: extraction, drying, tear-out of wet materials and repairs. On most burst-pipe jobs you need both, and the plumber usually comes first.

How can I tell if a pipe is frozen?

The Red Cross says that if you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, suspect a frozen pipe. Frozen pipes are most likely along exterior walls and where the water service enters through the foundation.

Does insurance cover a burst pipe?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe. Some exclude freeze damage if the home was left unheated or unattended without precautions. Policies differ, so check yours and report the loss quickly.

Can a burst pipe cause mold?

Yes. Water from a burst pipe often soaks into wall cavities, insulation and subfloors where you cannot see it. If those areas are not dried within 24 to 48 hours, as the EPA advises, mold can start to grow.
