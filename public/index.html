The water damage restoration process

Professional crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. The work usually runs in this order:

  1. Inspection and safety. The crew finds the source, checks for electrical hazards and decides the category of water (clean, gray or black) and the class of damage (how much of the room is wet and how deeply).
  2. Water removal. Submersible pumps and truck-mounted or portable extractors pull out standing water and wring water from carpet and pad.
  3. Removal of ruined materials. Wet carpet pad, swollen baseboards and drywall that wicked up water are cut out so the framing behind them can dry.
  4. Drying and dehumidification. Air movers push air across wet surfaces while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Moisture meters and thermal cameras track progress every day.
  5. Cleaning and disinfection. Surfaces touched by contaminated water are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.
  6. Repairs. New drywall, paint, flooring and trim go in once readings show the structure is dry.

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

There is no honest flat price for water damage, because no two losses are the same. These are the factors that move the number up or down:

  • Size of the wet area. One bathroom costs far less to dry than a finished basement.
  • Category of water. Sewage and floodwater need protective gear, disinfection and more material removal than clean water.
  • Materials involved. Hardwood, plaster and insulation are slower to dry and harder to save than tile or concrete.
  • How long the water sat. Water left for days spreads further and can grow mold, which adds remediation.
  • Drying time. Equipment is usually billed per day, so a 5-day dry costs more than a 3-day dry.
  • Repairs and rebuild. Replacing cabinets or flooring adds to the cost of drying.

Ask for a written estimate before work beyond emergency water removal begins. If you are filing a claim, your insurance company may also send an adjuster to review the scope.

Warning signs of hidden water damage

Not all water damage shows up as a puddle. Call for an inspection if you notice any of these:

  • Brown or yellow stains on ceilings or walls
  • Paint that bubbles, peels or feels soft
  • Warped, cupped or buckling floorboards
  • A musty smell that will not go away
  • Black or green spots on drywall, grout or caulk
  • A water bill that jumps without a reason
  • Damp carpet near a toilet, tub, washer or water heater

What to do before the crew arrives

  1. Stay safe. Do not walk into standing water if the power may be on. Shut off electricity to wet rooms only from a dry spot.
  2. Stop the water. Close the main shut-off valve for plumbing leaks, or the supply valve behind the toilet, sink or washer.
  3. Move what you can. Lift furniture onto blocks or foil, and move rugs, electronics and papers to a dry room.
  4. Take photos and call your insurer. Photograph the water, the source and damaged items before cleanup, then report the loss.

Water damage restoration questions

What is water damage restoration?

Water damage restoration is the work of returning a wet building to its condition before the loss. It covers finding the source, removing standing water, drying the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, cleaning and disinfecting, preventing mold, and repairing damaged drywall, flooring and trim.

How fast should water damage be dried?

As fast as possible. The EPA says water-damaged areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Drying a whole structure often takes several days, depending on how much water there is and what got wet; the crew confirms dryness with moisture readings.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or an overflowing washer. They usually exclude gradual leaks and wear and tear. Flooding from outside is not covered by most homeowners policies; FEMA notes it needs separate flood insurance. Check your own policy and talk with your agent.

Can I clean up water damage myself?

Small spills of clean water on hard floors can often be dried with towels and fans. Call a professional when water has soaked into carpet, drywall or insulation, when it came from a toilet, sewer or floodwater, when more than one room is wet, or when you notice a musty smell.

How much does water damage restoration cost?

The price depends on how much area is wet, the category of water, which materials must be removed and how many days the drying equipment runs. The crew should give you a written estimate after the inspection and before work beyond emergency water removal starts.

Do you work with insurance companies?

Yes. The crew documents the damage with photos and moisture readings, which your insurance company will ask for. You stay in charge of your claim, and you should report the loss to your insurer as soon as you can.
