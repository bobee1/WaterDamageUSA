Does Renters Insurance Cover Water Damage? Tenant vs. Landlord Responsibilities

By DryFlare Editorial · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Yes, renters insurance covers water damage to your belongings when the cause is sudden and accidental, like a burst pipe or an upstairs overflow. It does not cover the building, which is the landlord's job. It also does not cover flood; FloodSmart says a separate NFIP contents policy can cover up to $100,000 of a renter's belongings.

A leak in a rental raises two questions: who fixes the apartment, and who pays for your things. The landlord handles the building. Your renters policy, called an HO-4, handles your belongings. If the unit needs drying fast, DryFlare can help with water damage repair and drying in coordination with your landlord.

This is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Read your policy and lease, and ask your agent about your coverage.

What does renters insurance cover after water damage?

Renters insurance pays to repair or replace your belongings and may pay extra living costs if you have to move out for a while. It applies when the water damage comes from a covered, sudden event.

Usually covered by renters insuranceUsually not covered
Your furniture, clothes and electronics damaged by a burst pipeThe walls, floors and ceilings (landlord's policy)
Damage from an upstairs unit's overflowFlood from rising water outside
A hotel stay if the unit is unlivable (loss of use)Sewer backup, unless you add coverage
Liability if your overflow damages a neighbor's unitDamage from a slow leak you ignored

Personal property coverage pays either actual cash value (minus wear) or replacement cost. Replacement cost pays more but costs a little more.

Does renters insurance cover water leak damage?

Yes, when the leak is sudden. A pipe that bursts in the wall, a supply hose that splits or a toilet that overflows upstairs all usually count. A drip under the sink that you knew about for months does not. Report leaks to your landlord in writing as soon as you see them, both to get them fixed and to protect your claim.

Who pays: tenant or landlord?

The landlord's policy pays to repair the building. Your policy pays for your things. Here's how it usually splits.

ItemWho usually pays
Burst pipe repairLandlord
Drywall, ceiling and flooringLandlord
Water removal and drying of the unitLandlord
Your couch, bed, clothes and laptopYou (renters insurance)
Hotel while repairs happenYou (loss of use coverage), if you have it
Damage you caused to a neighbor's unitYou (liability coverage)

Leases and state laws vary. If your landlord ignored a leak you reported, they may owe you for the damage. Your state's tenant laws decide that, and a local tenant group or attorney can help.

Why renters need separate flood coverage

Renters insurance doesn't cover flood. FloodSmart notes that the NFIP sells a contents-only policy for renters, and that it isn't renters insurance; it only protects belongings in a flood. Coverage is for units above the lowest elevated floor. Basement units have tighter limits, so ask before you buy. As with all NFIP policies, there is usually a 30-day wait.

What to do when water damages your rental

  1. Stay safe. Keep away from water near outlets or a sagging ceiling.
  2. Tell the landlord right away, by phone and in writing. Ask them to shut off the water if you can't.
  3. Move your belongings to a dry room and get wet items up off the floor.
  4. Take photos and video of the damage and your things before you throw anything out.
  5. Call your renters insurer to open a claim and ask about loss of use.
  6. Keep receipts for anything you buy, like towels, fans or a hotel night.

Mold, health and moving out

The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. If your landlord doesn't start drying, the risk grows each day. Write down when you reported the leak and what was done. A ceiling stain that keeps spreading is a warning sign; see our guide to a water stain on the ceiling for what it can mean.

How renters can lower their risk

  • Buy renters insurance with replacement cost and loss of use coverage.
  • Add water backup coverage if you live in a basement or garden unit.
  • Buy NFIP contents coverage if floods can reach your building.
  • Keep valuables and electronics off the floor in basement units.
  • Learn where the unit's water shut-off valve is on day one.

Local note: Basement apartments are common in New York and New Jersey, where Ida's floods in 2021 showed the risk. Renters in Washington, DC English basements and older Massachusetts three-deckers also see leaks from the units above.

When should you call a water damage professional?

In a rental, the landlord usually hires the restoration company. Call one, or push your landlord to, when:

  • Carpet, walls or ceilings are soaked
  • Water came from a toilet, sewer or outside
  • The leak has run for more than a day
  • You smell mold or see stains spreading

Renters insurance water damage questions

Does renters insurance cover water leak damage from an upstairs neighbor?

Usually yes, for your belongings. Your renters policy pays for your damaged items, and the landlord's policy pays to repair the building. If the neighbor was careless, the insurers may sort out who pays later.

Is my landlord responsible for water damage to my things?

Usually not, unless the landlord was negligent, such as ignoring a leak you reported. The landlord's policy covers the building, not tenants' belongings. That's why renters insurance matters.

Does renters insurance pay for a hotel if my apartment is unlivable?

Many renters policies include loss of use or additional living expenses. If a covered water loss makes the unit unlivable, it may pay for a hotel and extra costs up to the policy limit.

Does renters insurance cover a burst pipe?

Generally yes, for your belongings, because a burst pipe is sudden and accidental. The landlord's insurance handles the pipe and the building repairs.
