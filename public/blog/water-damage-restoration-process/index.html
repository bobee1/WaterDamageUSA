What Is Water Damage Restoration? The Process Step by Step

By DryBeacon Editorial · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Water damage restoration is the process of returning a wet building to its normal condition. It runs in order: inspection, water extraction, removing wet materials, drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, cleaning, and repairs. Drying is the key step, and the EPA advises getting wet materials dry within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

If you've never dealt with water damage before, it helps to know what's coming. Crews arrive, equipment hums for days, walls may get opened up, and then the rebuild starts. Here is each step, in plain words. When you need it done, DryBeacon handles water damage restoration from the first call through repairs.

What is water damage restoration?

Water damage restoration covers everything needed to return a home or business to normal after a leak or flood. It has two halves. Mitigation stops the damage: extraction, tear-out and drying. Repair rebuilds what was removed. The industry follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which defines how to judge the water and how to dry a building.

The water damage restoration process, step by step

  1. Stop the water. Shut off the main valve or fix the source. A plumber or roofer may be needed first.
  2. Inspect and assess. The crew finds where water went, using moisture meters and often a thermal camera. They decide the water category and damage class.
  3. Protect contents. Furniture is moved or blocked up, and belongings are moved out of wet areas.
  4. Extract the water. Pumps and truck-mounted or portable extractors remove standing water from floors and carpet.
  5. Tear out wet materials. Soaked carpet pad, insulation and drywall that can't be dried are removed, often 1 to 2 feet above the wet line.
  6. Dry the structure. Air movers and dehumidifiers run, sometimes for days, until materials are dry.
  7. Clean and treat. Surfaces are cleaned, and antimicrobial products are used when needed, especially after gray or black water.
  8. Monitor daily. The crew takes moisture readings each day and adjusts equipment.
  9. Repair and rebuild. New drywall, paint, flooring, trim and cabinets go in once everything is dry.

Water categories: how dirty is the water?

The S500 standard sorts water into three categories. The category decides what can be saved.

CategoryExamplesWhat it means for cleanup
Category 1 (clean)Supply line break, sink overflowMany materials can be dried in place
Category 2 (gray)Washer or dishwasher dischargeCarpet pad and some porous items removed
Category 3 (black)Sewage, floodwater from outsidePorous materials removed; area disinfected; protective gear

The CDC says to discard drywall and insulation soaked by floodwater or sewage. Sewage needs its own steps; see sewage backup in the basement.

Damage classes: how wet is the room?

The class describes how much of a room got wet and how deeply water soaked in. Class 1 is a small area with little absorption. Class 4 means water soaked deep into dense materials like hardwood, plaster or concrete. Higher classes need more equipment and more time.

How drying works

Drying has three parts: moving air, removing moisture from that air, and sometimes adding heat. Air movers push air across wet surfaces so water evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air. The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, and crews go lower during drying. Our guide to dehumidifiers for water damage explains household vs. professional units.

Drying is done when wet materials match the moisture of dry ones in the same house, called the dry standard. Not when they feel dry.

Safety during restoration

  • Keep away from water near electrical outlets and cords.
  • Wear gloves, boots and an N95 mask around dirty water or mold, as the CDC advises.
  • Small mold areas, under about 10 square feet, can be cleaned at home, says the EPA. Bigger areas need a pro.
  • In homes built before 1978, the EPA warns that cutting or sanding old paint can release lead dust.

Restoration and insurance

Insurers expect you to take reasonable steps to limit damage, and restoration is that step. Crews document moisture readings and photos, which adjusters use. Price depends on size, water type and materials; see water damage restoration cost for what drives it.

How to prevent the next water damage job

  • Install leak sensors near water heaters, washers and sinks.
  • Consider an automatic shut-off valve on the main line.
  • Replace washer hoses and supply lines on a schedule.
  • Keep gutters clear and test your sump pump each season.
  • Know where your main water shut-off is.

Local note: The steps stay the same, but the cause changes by region: hurricanes and surge in Louisiana, frozen pipes in Minnesota, slab leaks in Arizona, and basement floods in Ohio.

When should you call a water damage restoration professional?

  • Water spread beyond one small area
  • Walls, ceilings, carpet or wood floors are wet
  • The water was gray or black
  • Water sat for more than 24 hours
  • You need documentation for an insurance claim

Water damage restoration process questions

What is the difference between water mitigation and water damage restoration?

Mitigation is the emergency phase: stopping the water, extraction, tear-out and drying. Restoration usually means the whole job, including repairs like new drywall, paint and flooring once the structure is dry.

How long does the water damage restoration process take?

Extraction usually takes hours. Drying often takes three to five days, longer for hardwood, plaster or concrete. Repairs can take days to weeks depending on materials and insurance approval.

Can I stay home during water damage restoration?

Often yes, for a clean-water leak in part of the home. The drying equipment runs day and night and is loud. For sewage, large floods or mold, staying elsewhere may be safer.

Do I need to be home while the crew works?

Someone should be there for the first visit to walk through the damage and sign paperwork. After that, the crew mainly needs access for daily moisture checks and equipment changes.
