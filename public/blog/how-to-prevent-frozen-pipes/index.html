How to Prevent Frozen Pipes: Steps Before, During and After a Cold Snap

By DryFlare Editorial · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: To prevent frozen pipes, keep the heat on (at least 55°F when you are away), open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls, let a faucet drip on the coldest nights, and insulate pipes in crawl spaces, attics and garages. Drain and shut off outdoor lines before winter. If a pipe freezes, thaw it slowly with gentle heat, never an open flame.

Frozen pipes are one of the most common causes of winter water damage. A split pipe can release gallons of water an hour, often in a wall or ceiling where no one sees it. The good news is that most freezes can be prevented with a few cheap steps. If a pipe has already burst, shut off the main and get help with burst pipe cleanup.

Why do pipes freeze and burst?

When water freezes, it expands. In a pipe, the ice builds a plug, and pressure rises in the water trapped between the ice and a closed faucet. That pressure, not the ice itself, is what usually splits the pipe. The crack may stay hidden until the ice melts and water starts to flow again.

The pipes most at risk are the ones closest to the cold:

  • Pipes in crawl spaces, unheated basements and garages
  • Pipes in outside walls, especially on the north side of the house
  • Lines in attics and under kitchen or bathroom cabinets on outside walls
  • Outdoor hose bibs, sprinkler lines and pool supply lines
  • Plumbing under mobile homes and in room additions with little insulation

How to prevent frozen pipes before winter

Most of the work happens in the fall, before the first hard freeze. These steps follow advice from the American Red Cross.

  1. Drain outdoor lines. Disconnect garden hoses, shut off the inside valve to outdoor faucets and open the outside tap to drain it. Drain sprinkler and pool supply lines as the maker directs.
  2. Insulate exposed pipes. Foam pipe sleeves are cheap and easy to fit in crawl spaces, basements, attics and garages. Heat tape or heat cable rated for pipes can help in the coldest spots; follow the instructions closely.
  3. Seal air leaks. Caulk gaps where pipes pass through outside walls, and close crawl space vents in deep cold if your home's design allows it.
  4. Find your main shutoff valve. Make sure everyone in the house knows where it is and that it turns. In a freeze emergency, minutes matter.
  5. Add insulation where it is thin. Attics, rim joists and walls with plumbing benefit most.

How to tell if a pipe is frozen

  • Little or no water comes out when you open a faucet
  • Frost on an exposed pipe, or a pipe that bulges
  • Odd smells from a drain, or gurgling sounds
  • Only one faucet or one side of the house has no water

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

Act fast, but gently. A frozen pipe can burst while you wait.

  1. Open the faucet on that line so melting water and pressure can escape.
  2. Apply gentle heat to the frozen section, starting nearest the faucet. The Red Cross lists a hair dryer, a heating pad, a space heater kept a safe distance away, or towels soaked in hot water.
  3. Never use an open flame, such as a torch, propane heater or charcoal stove. It is a fire risk, and it can damage the pipe.
  4. Keep heating until full water pressure returns, then check other faucets for more frozen spots.
  5. Call a plumber if you cannot find or reach the frozen part, or if it will not thaw.

What to do if a frozen pipe bursts

Shut off the main water valve right away. Turn off power to wet areas if you can do it safely. Then move belongings out of the way, take photos for your claim and start removing water. Our step-by-step guide on what to do when a pipe bursts walks through each step.

Speed matters after the leak stops. The EPA says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Water in walls, under floors and in insulation is hard to find and dry without moisture meters and drying gear, which is where professional drying helps.

Does insurance cover frozen pipes?

Often, when the damage is sudden. The NAIC explains that homeowners policies generally cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe. But they also say policies may require you to keep the home heated, or shut off the water and drain the pipes, if it is empty. A claim for a vacant, unheated house may be denied. When should you call a professional?

  • A pipe burst or water got into walls, ceilings or floors
  • You cannot find or reach the frozen section
  • Water reached electrical outlets, fixtures or the panel
  • Wet drywall, carpet or insulation has been wet for more than a day
  • You smell mold after a past freeze

Frozen pipe questions

At what temperature do pipes freeze?

Water freezes at 32°F, but pipes inside a heated home rarely freeze at that point. The risk climbs when it stays well below freezing for hours, especially for pipes in outside walls, crawl spaces, garages and attics. Wind and poor insulation make it worse.

Should I let my faucets drip at night?

Yes, on the coldest nights. The American Red Cross says a trickle of cold water from a faucet fed by exposed pipes can help keep them from freezing. Moving water is harder to freeze, and an open tap lets pressure escape.

What temperature should I leave my house at in winter?

The Red Cross advises keeping the heat at no lower than 55°F if you will be away. Keep the thermostat at the same setting day and night during a cold snap instead of turning it down while you sleep.

Can a frozen pipe burst after it thaws?

Yes. Ice can split a pipe, and the leak often starts when the ice melts and water flows again. That is why you should watch a thawed pipe closely and know where your main shutoff valve is.

Does insurance cover frozen pipes?

Often, if the damage was sudden. But the NAIC notes that policies usually expect you to keep the home heated or shut off and drain the water if it is vacant. Damage in a house left unheated may be denied.
