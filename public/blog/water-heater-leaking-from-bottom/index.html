Water Heater Leaking From the Bottom: Causes, Dangers and What to Do

By DryBeacon Editorial · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: A water heater leaking from the bottom is usually caused by a loose or faulty drain valve, a dripping relief valve, condensation, or a tank that has rusted through. A drain valve or relief valve can often be fixed. A leak from the tank itself can't be repaired, and the water heater needs replacing.

Finding a puddle under your water heater is stressful, and it's easy to wonder if the tank is about to burst. Most leaks start small, so you usually have time to act. If water has already spread into walls, flooring or a finished basement, DryBeacon can send a crew for water extraction and drying while your plumber deals with the heater.

This guide covers tank-style water heaters. It is general information; always follow the safety instructions on your unit's label.

Why is my water heater leaking from the bottom?

Most water heater leaks at the base come from one of five sources. Find the source before you decide on a fix.

CauseWhat you'll seeUsual fix
Loose or worn drain valveDrips from the valve near the baseTighten it, or have a plumber replace it
T&P valve releasing waterWater running down the discharge pipe to the floorCheck water pressure and the expansion tank
CondensationSmall puddle on gas units, often when filled with cold waterUsually stops as the tank warms up
Sediment and corrosionRust-colored water, rumbling sounds, a wet baseFlushing may help early; a leaking tank needs replacing
Cracked or rusted tankSteady leak from under the jacketReplace the water heater

The drain valve

The drain valve sits near the bottom of the tank. It's used to flush sediment. Plastic valves can wear out, and any valve can work loose. If the drip stops when you snug it gently with a wrench, you've found the problem.

The temperature and pressure relief valve

The T&P valve opens to release water if the tank gets too hot or the pressure gets too high. Its discharge pipe runs down the side of the tank, so its water often ends up on the floor. A. O. Smith notes that a dripping relief valve often means water pressure is too high or a closed system needs an expansion tank. Don't plug or cap this valve. It's a safety device.

Sediment and a failing tank

Minerals in the water settle at the bottom of the tank. Over time they trap heat and speed up corrosion. The anode rod inside the tank slows rust, but once it's used up, the steel starts to corrode. When the tank rusts through, the leak comes from under the outer jacket and can't be fixed.

What to do if your water heater is leaking from the bottom

  1. Turn off the power or gas. For an electric heater, switch off the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to "off" or "pilot."
  2. Shut off the cold water supply. Close the valve on the cold inlet pipe at the top of the tank. This stops more water from entering.
  3. Find the source. Dry the area, then watch where new drops appear: valve, fittings, discharge pipe or the base of the tank.
  4. Protect the floor. Move boxes and rugs, set out towels or a pan, and use a wet-dry vacuum on standing water.
  5. Drain the tank if it's the tank. Attach a hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or outside. Let the water cool first; it can scald.
  6. Take photos. Photograph the heater, the label and the damage before cleanup. Your insurer may ask for them.

Is a leaking water heater dangerous?

A small leak is not usually an emergency, but it can become one. The bigger risks are water damage, mold and, rarely, a failed relief valve. A tank holds 30 to 80 gallons, and it keeps refilling while the supply valve is open. That can soak flooring, drywall and anything stored nearby.

  • Water damage: slow leaks wick into drywall and subfloors you can't see.
  • Mold: the EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.
  • Electrical risk: keep away from water near outlets, cords or the heater's wiring.
  • Pressure: never block a relief valve that is releasing water. Call a plumber to find out why.

Repair or replace: what fixes a leaking water heater?

Fittings and valves can be repaired. A leaking tank needs to be replaced. Use this as a quick guide, and let a plumber confirm.

Leak locationRepair or replace?
Drain valveRepair: tighten or replace the valve
Inlet or outlet fittings on topRepair: tighten or replace the connectors
T&P valve or discharge pipeRepair: check pressure, expansion tank or valve
Base of the tank or under the jacketReplace the water heater

Gas water heaters, venting and new installs often need a permit and a licensed plumber in many cities. Check local rules before you start.

Water damage from a leaking water heater

Once the leak is stopped, the cleanup starts. Water from a tank is clean at first, but it can pick up contaminants as it sits on floors. Water heaters are often in garages, closets, basements and attics. An attic or upstairs leak can bring water through the ceiling below.

A restoration crew removes the water, checks walls and floors with moisture meters, and dries the area with air movers and dehumidifiers. Does insurance cover a leaking water heater?

Usually, homeowners insurance covers sudden water damage from a water heater, but not the heater itself. The NAIC explains that policies cover damage from a "sudden and accidental" discharge from plumbing. A tank that wore out from age is treated as maintenance. A slow leak that went on for weeks may also be denied. Read more in does homeowners insurance cover water damage.

How to prevent water heater leaks

  • Flush the tank yearly to clear sediment, as most manufacturers advise.
  • Check the anode rod every few years and replace it when it's worn.
  • Test the T&P valve once a year by following the label instructions.
  • Control water pressure. A. O. Smith suggests a pressure reducing valve set to 50 to 60 psi.
  • Add a drain pan under the tank, piped to a drain, and a leak sensor.
  • Know the shut-off. Everyone in the house should know where the cold supply valve is.

Local note: Hard water speeds up sediment and tank wear in much of the Southwest, so owners in Arizona, Nevada and Utah should flush tanks more often. In Florida and Texas, heaters are often in garages and attics, where a leak can go unnoticed.

When should you call a water damage professional?

Call a plumber for the water heater itself. Call a restoration company when the water has gone beyond what towels and a wet-dry vacuum can handle.

  • Water has spread into drywall, carpet or under flooring
  • The heater is in an attic or upstairs and the ceiling below is wet
  • Water sat for more than a day before you found it
  • You smell mustiness or see stains spreading
  • A finished basement or several rooms are wet

Water heater leak questions

Who should I call for a leaking water heater?

Call a plumber to repair or replace the water heater. If water has spread across floors, into walls or into a finished basement, call a water damage restoration company too. They remove the water and dry the structure so mold doesn't start.

How do I fix a leaking water heater tank?

You can't. A leak from the steel tank itself means the lining has corroded through, and the tank must be replaced. Leaks from valves, fittings or the drain valve can often be fixed.

Can I still use hot water if my water heater is leaking?

Only if the leak is from a fitting and it's minor, and only until a plumber can fix it. If the tank itself is leaking, shut off the water and power or gas. Using it can make the leak worse.

Will a leaking water heater cause mold?

It can. Water that soaks drywall, wood or carpet for more than 24 to 48 hours can grow mold, according to the EPA. Dry the area fully, not just the surface.
