New York is an old city: 38.2% of its homes predate 1940, so cast-iron drains and century-old basements show up on many jobs.

Why water damage in New York is different

Nearly four in five New York City homes were built before 1980. Old risers, steam heat and cast-iron drains leak in co-ops, rentals and brownstones, and one leak can soak every apartment below. In the Rockaways, Red Hook and Lower Manhattan, Sandy's surge in 2012 flooded thousands of buildings.

Flash floods are a growing risk. In September 2021, the remnants of Ida flooded basement apartments in Queens, and people died. On September 29, 2023, a record rainstorm flooded subways and streets in Brooklyn and Queens. Storm drains can't keep up when several inches fall in an hour.

Water damage tips for New York homes

  • Basement tenants should have a plan to get out fast in flash floods.
  • Know which pipes belong to your unit and which to the building.
  • Sign up for Notify NYC alerts.
  • Keep valuables off the floor in ground-floor units.

Water damage questions in New York

Do you cover all of New York?

Yes. DryBeacon takes calls from across New York and New York City's five boroughs, including the Upper West Side, Harlem, Park Slope, Astoria and the Rockaways, and ZIP codes such as 10001, 10025, 10128, 11201, 11215, 11375.

How fast should water damage be dried in New York?

As soon as possible. For New York homes, the EPA advice is to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold.

Does insurance cover water damage in New York?

For New York homeowners, sudden events like burst pipes are usually covered. Flood from rising water needs a separate flood policy. For claim problems, contact the New York Department of Financial Services.

Are basement apartments in New York safe from flooding?

Not always. Ida in 2021 showed that basement units can flood within minutes in extreme rain. Tenants should know two ways out, move valuables up and consider NFIP contents coverage.
