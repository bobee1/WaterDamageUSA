Why water damage in Missouri is different

The Missouri and Mississippi rivers meet near St. Louis, and both flood in wet years. The Great Flood of 1993 is still the benchmark, and 2019 brought major flooding again. In July 2022, St. Louis got about nine inches of rain in a few hours, flooding homes and roads across the city and county.

Severe storms are Missouri's most common disaster, with 38 FEMA declarations since 2000. The 2011 Joplin tornado destroyed thousands of homes. Many older homes in Kansas City and St. Louis have basements that take water in heavy rain. Kansas and Illinois share many of these risks.

Insurance and licensing in Missouri

The Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance regulates home insurance in Missouri. It takes complaints if a claim stalls. Missouri homeowners policies cover wind and hail, but river and flash flooding need a separate flood policy.

Flood needs its own policy. Owners in Missouri can buy one from the NFIP or a private carrier. NFIP cover starts after a 30-day wait.

Missouri has no statewide general contractor license. Kansas City, St. Louis and other cities license contractors locally. Ask which local license applies.

Water damage questions in Missouri

Do you cover all of Missouri?

Yes. DryBeacon takes calls from all of Missouri, including Kansas City, St. Louis, Springfield, Columbia and Independence. Small towns and rural areas in every county are covered too.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage in Missouri?

Usually not. Most home policies in Missouri exclude rising water. They often cover sudden leaks, like a burst pipe. Flood needs a separate NFIP or private policy. For claim help, contact the Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance.

Does a water damage company need a license in Missouri?

Missouri has no statewide general contractor license, so local rules apply. Plumbing and electrical work still need licensed trades, and your city may require permits for rebuilds.

Is flash flooding covered by homeowners insurance in Missouri?

No. Flash flooding counts as flood, which standard policies exclude. You need a flood policy through the NFIP or a private insurer. Water from a burst pipe or roof leak is usually covered.
