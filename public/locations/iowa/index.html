Why water damage in Iowa is different

The 2008 floods put much of downtown Cedar Rapids under water, and Iowa City was hit too. In 2019, the Missouri River flooded towns in western Iowa. In June 2024, the Rock and Big Sioux rivers flooded Rock Valley and other towns in northwest Iowa. FEMA has declared 41 water and storm disasters in Iowa since 2000.

Wind is the other threat. The August 2020 derecho tore through Cedar Rapids and central Iowa, damaging roofs across the region. Iowa's housing is old, with almost a quarter of homes built before 1940. Many have stone or block basements that seep in wet weather. Nebraska and Minnesota share Iowa's river risks.

Tips for Iowa homeowners

  • Know your flood zone; many river towns updated maps after 2008.
  • Keep a written list of what is stored in your basement for claims.
  • Seal cracks in basement walls and keep gutters clean.
  • Leave heat on and pipes insulated in farm outbuildings with water lines.

Insurance and licensing in Iowa

The Iowa Insurance Division regulates home insurance in Iowa. It takes complaints if a claim stalls. Iowa homeowners policies cover wind and hail, but rising river water needs a separate flood policy.

Most home policies exclude flood. In Iowa, flood cover comes from FEMA's flood program (NFIP) or a private insurer. NFIP policies usually take 30 days to start.

Contractors who work in Iowa must register with the Iowa Division of Labor. Plumbing, HVAC and electrical work need state trade licenses.

Water damage questions in Iowa

Do you cover all of Iowa?

Yes. DryFlare takes calls from all of Iowa, including Des Moines, Cedar Rapids, Davenport, Sioux City and Iowa City. Small towns and rural areas in every county are covered too.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage in Iowa?

Usually not. Most home policies in Iowa exclude rising water. They often cover sudden leaks, like a burst pipe. Flood needs a separate NFIP or private policy. For claim help, contact the Iowa Insurance Division.

Does a water damage company need a license in Iowa?

Rules depend on the work. Check builders with the Iowa Division of Labor (contractor registration). Plumbing and electrical work need trade licenses.

How long does it take to dry a flooded basement in Iowa?

Most basements take three to five days with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. Older stone foundations and concrete can take longer. Crews check with moisture meters and stop when materials reach a dry reading.
