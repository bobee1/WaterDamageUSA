Why water damage in Montana is different

Montana's cold snaps can push temperatures far below zero. Pipes freeze in cabins, crawl spaces and second homes that sit empty for weeks. A pipe that splits in an empty cabin can run for days before anyone finds it.

Spring melt and rain-on-snow cause floods. In June 2022, the Yellowstone River and its creeks flooded Red Lodge, Gardiner and Livingston. Ice jams on the Missouri and Yellowstone can also push water into towns in late winter. Wyoming and Idaho share these mountain risks.

Tips for Montana homeowners

  • Winterize cabins by draining the lines or keeping heat at 55°F or higher.
  • Install a smart leak sensor and auto shut-off in second homes.
  • Clear snow from roofs and away from foundations before spring.
  • Check FEMA flood maps if you live near a river.

Insurance and licensing in Montana

The Montana Commissioner of Securities and Insurance oversees home insurance in Montana. It can help with claim problems. Many Montana policies limit freeze claims in unheated or vacant homes, so read the terms for cabins and seasonal homes.

Most home policies in Montana leave out rising water. A flood policy from the NFIP or a private insurer fills the gap. NFIP policies have a 30-day wait.

Contractors with employees must register with the Montana Department of Labor and Industry. Plumbers and electricians need state licenses, and some cities add local rules.

Water damage questions in Montana

Do you cover all of Montana?

Yes. DryBeacon takes calls from all of Montana, including Billings, Missoula, Great Falls, Bozeman and Butte. Small towns and rural areas in every county are covered too.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage in Montana?

Usually not. Most home policies in Montana exclude rising water. They often cover sudden leaks, like a burst pipe. Flood needs a separate NFIP or private policy. For claim help, contact the Montana Commissioner of Securities and Insurance.

Does a water damage company need a license in Montana?

Rules depend on the work. Check builders with the Montana Department of Labor and Industry (contractor registration). Plumbing and electrical work need trade licenses.

How do I protect a Montana cabin from frozen pipes?

Drain the plumbing and water heater when you close it for winter, or keep heat on at 55°F or higher. A remote temperature sensor can alert you if the heat fails. Shut off the main water valve when you leave.
