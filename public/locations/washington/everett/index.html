Housing in Everett is newer, with a median build year of 1981. Supply lines, water heaters and appliances cause most indoor leaks.

Why water damage in Everett is different

About 16% of Everett homes were built before 1940, many in North Everett, Riverside and Bayside. These houses often have old basements, original drain lines and wood framing that has seen decades of damp winters. Water creeps in through foundation cracks and around old basement windows. Tree roots also break into clay sewer lines and cause backups after heavy rain.

The Snohomish River wraps around the north and east sides of the city. In big winter storms, the river and its sloughs can rise and flood low ground in the Lowell area and the river valley. Up on the hills, steep yards send runoff toward houses. About 3% of homes are mobile homes, where leaks under the floor can go unseen and rot the subfloor.

Tips for Everett homeowners

  • Regrade soil so it slopes away from the foundation.
  • Have an old clay sewer line scoped before it fails.
  • Check under a manufactured home for drips twice a year.
  • Sign up for Snohomish County flood alerts if you live near the river.

Water damage questions in Everett

Do you cover all of Everett?

Yes. DryFlare takes calls from across Everett and Snohomish County, including North Everett, Riverside, Bayside, Delta and Lowell, and ZIP codes such as 98201, 98203, 98204, 98208.

How fast should water damage be dried in Everett?

Start within 24 to 48 hours. The EPA says that is the window to dry wet materials before mold grows in a Everett home.

Does insurance cover water damage in Everett?

For Everett homeowners, sudden events like burst pipes are usually covered. Flood from rising water needs a separate flood policy. For claim problems, contact the Washington Office of the Insurance Commissioner.

Is flood insurance worth it in Everett?

If you live in a low area near the Snohomish River or its sloughs, it is worth pricing. Standard home insurance does not cover rising water. Even outside mapped flood zones, heavy rain can flood low lots. Policies usually take 30 days to start, so do not wait for a storm.
