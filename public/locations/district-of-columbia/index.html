Why water damage in Washington, DC is different

Nearly a third of DC homes were built before 1940, more than in any state. Rowhouses share walls, so a leak in one unit can travel to the next. English basements sit partly below ground and flood when storm drains back up. In older neighborhoods, the combined sewer system carries both storm water and sewage, and heavy rain can overwhelm it.

Flash floods are real here. In June 2006, heavy rain flooded the Federal Triangle and several museums, and a July 2019 storm dropped more than three inches in an hour. The Potomac and Anacostia rivers also rise in big storms. Neighboring Maryland and Virginia share the region's storms.

Tips for property owners in Washington, DC

  • Install a backwater valve if your basement has a floor drain or bathroom.
  • Keep window wells and stairwell drains clear of leaves.
  • In a condo, keep your HO-6 policy up to date and know your building's insurance rules.
  • Ask your landlord or condo board where the main water shut-off is.

Insurance and licensing in Washington, DC

The DC Department of Insurance, Securities and Banking oversees home insurance in the District of Columbia. It can help with claim problems. Renters in DC basement units should carry renters insurance; the landlord's policy covers the building, not the tenant's belongings.

Most home policies in the District of Columbia leave out rising water. A flood policy from the NFIP or a private insurer fills the gap. NFIP policies have a 30-day wait.

The DC Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection licenses home improvement contractors, and DC requires permits for most rebuild work. the District of Columbia also licenses mold work through the DC Department of Energy and Environment (DOEE) mold licensing. If mold is part of the job, ask for that license too.

Water damage questions in Washington, DC

Do you cover all of the District of Columbia?

Yes. DryBeacon takes calls from every ward of the District, from Capitol Hill and Georgetown to Anacostia, and from nearby Maryland and Virginia suburbs.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage in the District of Columbia?

Usually not. Most home policies in the District of Columbia exclude rising water. They often cover sudden leaks, like a burst pipe. Flood needs a separate NFIP or private policy. For claim help, contact the DC Department of Insurance, Securities and Banking.

Does a water damage company need a license in the District of Columbia?

Yes. Home improvement contractors in DC need a license from the Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection, and plumbing and electrical work need licensed trades. Mold assessment and remediation are licensed separately through the DC Department of Energy and Environment (DOEE) mold licensing.

Who fixes water damage in a DC rental?

The landlord is usually responsible for repairing the building and fixing leaks. Tenants are responsible for their own belongings, which renters insurance can cover. Report leaks to the landlord in writing right away.
