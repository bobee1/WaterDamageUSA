Why water damage in Columbia is different

In October 2015, a stalled storm dropped record rain on the Midlands. About 16.6 inches fell near Gills Creek in a single day. The creek rose to 17.08 feet before its gauge failed, far above its old record of 9.43 feet. Several dams in the Gills Creek watershed broke, and some homes along the creek flooded to the eaves.

That flood showed how fast water can move through Columbia's creek valleys. Day to day, the threats are smaller but constant. Summer thunderstorms and tropical remnants bring heavy rain. The heat and humidity mean wet drywall can grow mold within a day or two. Older neighborhoods like Shandon, Elmwood Park and Earlewood have aging roofs, plumbing and crawl spaces.

How Columbia owners can prevent water damage

  • Check whether your home is near Gills Creek or another mapped floodplain.
  • Buy flood insurance early; it usually takes 30 days to start.
  • Run AC or a dehumidifier after any leak to keep humidity down.
  • Renters: report leaks to your landlord in writing right away.

Water damage questions in Columbia

Do you cover all of Columbia?

Yes. DryFlare takes calls from across Columbia and Richland County, including Shandon, Rosewood, Five Points, The Vista and Elmwood Park, and ZIP codes such as 29201, 29203, 29204, 29205, 29206, 29209.

How fast should water damage be dried in Columbia?

Start within 24 to 48 hours. The EPA says that is the window to dry wet materials before mold grows in a Columbia home.

Does insurance cover water damage in Columbia?

For Columbia homeowners, sudden events like burst pipes are usually covered. Flood from rising water needs a separate flood policy. For claim problems, contact the South Carolina Department of Insurance.

Could a 2015-style flood happen again in Columbia?

It could. Dams in the Gills Creek watershed have been repaired or reviewed, but extreme rain can still flood creek valleys. Homes near creeks should carry flood insurance, keep valuables off lower floors and know how to leave quickly.
