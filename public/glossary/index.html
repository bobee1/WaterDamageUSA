Water Damage Glossary

Restoration crews, plumbers and adjusters use a lot of jargon. Here is what the most common water damage terms mean, in plain words.

  • 55 terms
  • A–Z
  • Linked to full guides

Quick answer: This water damage glossary defines 55 terms you may hear during a leak, a flood or an insurance claim. Each definition links to the guide or service page that covers it in depth. Use the letters below to jump to a term.

ABCDEFGHILMNPRSTWX

A

ACV (actual cash value)
What an item was worth when it was damaged: replacement cost minus wear and age. Many policies pay ACV unless you have replacement cost cover. Homeowners insurance and water damage
Air mover
A high-speed fan that blows air across wet floors and walls to speed up evaporation. Restoration crews place them in a pattern around the room. Water mitigation and drying
Air scrubber
A portable machine that pulls room air through a HEPA filter to catch mold spores and dust during cleanup. Mold remediation
Anode rod
A metal rod inside a tank water heater that corrodes in place of the tank. A worn-out rod lets the tank rust and leak. Water heater leaking from the bottom
Antimicrobial
A cleaner that kills or slows bacteria and mold on surfaces after water damage. Sewage cleanup

B

Backwater valve
A one-way valve on a home's sewer line that lets waste flow out but closes if sewage tries to flow back in. Sewage backup in the basement
Black water
Category 3 water: highly contaminated water such as sewage or floodwater from rivers. It needs protective gear and disinfection. Sewage cleanup

C

Category 1 water
Clean water from a sanitary source, such as a broken supply line. It can turn into Category 2 or 3 if it sits. Water damage restoration process
Category 2 water
Gray water that holds some contamination, such as washing machine or dishwasher discharge. Water damage restoration process
Category 3 water
Black water that is grossly contaminated, such as sewage, toilet backflow with feces, or floodwater from outside. Sewage cleanup
Class of water damage
A scale from Class 1 to 4 in the IICRC S500 standard. It rates how much of a room got wet and how much water soaked in. It sets how much drying gear a job needs. Water mitigation
Containment
Plastic sheeting and air pressure control that keep dust, mold or sewage inside the work area. Mold remediation
Crawl space
The shallow space under a home with a raised floor. It traps moisture and is a common place for hidden water damage. Water mitigation

D

Deductible
The amount you pay on a claim before insurance pays. Many policies have a separate, higher deductible for wind, hail or hurricanes. Homeowners insurance and water damage
Dehumidifier
A machine that removes moisture from the air. Restoration crews use low-grain refrigerant (LGR) or desiccant units that dry air far more than household models. Dehumidifiers for water damage
Desiccant dehumidifier
A dehumidifier that pulls water from the air with a drying material instead of cold coils. It works well in cold spaces and dense materials. Dehumidifiers for water damage
Dry standard
The moisture reading of a dry, unaffected material in the same building. Drying is done when wet materials match it. Water damage restoration process
Drywall
Gypsum board used for most walls and ceilings. It wicks water upward and often has to be cut out after a leak. Water damage repair

E

Efflorescence
White, chalky mineral deposits left on concrete or brick when water passes through and evaporates. It is a sign of moisture. Sump pump failure
Expansion tank
A small tank on the water heater line that absorbs pressure as water heats. A failed one can make the relief valve drip. Water heater leaking from the bottom

F

Flood (insurance)
For insurance, flood means water covering normally dry land, such as a river rising or storm surge. Standard home policies leave it out. Flood damage restoration
Flood zone
An area FEMA maps for flood risk. High-risk zones start with A or V, and lenders require flood insurance there for federally backed mortgages. Flood damage restoration
Frozen pipe
A water line that has iced up. The pressure that builds between the ice and a closed faucet is what usually splits the pipe. What to do when a pipe bursts

G

Gray water
Category 2 water with some contamination, such as washer or dishwasher discharge or a toilet overflow with urine only. Water damage restoration process

H

HEPA filter
A high-efficiency air filter. It traps at least 99.97% of particles 0.3 microns in size. Air scrubbers and HEPA vacuums use it. Mold remediation
HO-3 policy
The most common home insurance form. It covers the house against any risk that isn't excluded, and your things against listed risks. Homeowners insurance and water damage
HO-4 policy
Renters insurance. It covers the tenant's belongings and liability, not the building. Renters insurance and water damage
HO-6 policy
Condo insurance. It covers the inside of the unit, improvements and belongings, while the HOA's master policy covers the building. Ceiling water damage repair
Hydrostatic pressure
The force of groundwater pushing against basement walls and floors. It drives water through cracks and joints. Sump pump failure

I

Ice dam
A ridge of ice at the roof edge that traps melting snow. The water backs up under shingles and leaks into ceilings and walls. Ceiling water damage repair
IICRC S500
The ANSI/IICRC standard for professional water damage restoration. It defines water categories, damage classes and drying practice. Water damage restoration process
Infrared camera
A thermal camera that shows heat differences. Wet spots read cooler, so it helps find hidden water in walls. Water damage restoration process

L

LGR dehumidifier
A low-grain refrigerant dehumidifier. It can dry air to much lower humidity than a household unit, which speeds up structural drying. Dehumidifiers for water damage

M

Mitigation
The emergency steps that stop damage from getting worse: stopping the water, extraction, tear-out and drying. Water mitigation
Moisture meter
A tool that measures how much water is in wood, drywall or concrete. Readings show where water spread and when drying is done. Water damage restoration process
Mold
A fungus that grows on damp materials. Indoor mold can start within 24 to 48 hours after water damage if materials stay wet. Mold remediation

N

NFIP
The National Flood Insurance Program, run by FEMA. It sells flood policies through insurers and agents, usually with a 30-day wait. Flood damage restoration

P

Personal property coverage
The part of a home or renters policy that pays for your things, like furniture, clothes and electronics. Renters insurance and water damage
Pressure reducing valve
A valve where the water main enters the home that lowers street pressure. High pressure strains pipes, hoses and water heaters. What to do when a pipe bursts

R

Relative humidity
How much moisture the air holds, compared with the most it could hold. The EPA advises keeping it below 60%, ideally 30 to 50%. Dehumidifiers for water damage
Remediation
Removing and cleaning up a hazard, such as mold or sewage, after the cause is fixed. Mold remediation
Replacement cost
Insurance that pays to replace a damaged item with a new one of similar kind, without taking off for age. Homeowners insurance and water damage

S

Sewer lateral
The pipe that carries waste from a house to the city sewer main. In many cities the owner is responsible for it. Sewage backup in the basement
Slab leak
A leak in a water line under a concrete slab foundation. It can wet flooring for weeks before anyone notices. Water damage repair
Structural drying
Drying the building itself, such as framing, subfloor and concrete, with air movers, dehumidifiers and monitoring. Water mitigation and structural drying
Subfloor
The plywood or board layer under finished flooring. It can trap water and swell after a leak. Water damage repair
Sudden and accidental
Insurance wording for damage that happens fast and by surprise, like a burst pipe. Slow leaks and wear usually don't count. Homeowners insurance and water damage
Sump pump
A pump in a basement pit that sends groundwater away from the home before it rises over the floor. Sump pump failure

T

T&P valve
The temperature and pressure relief valve on a water heater. It opens to release water if tank heat or pressure gets too high. Water heater leaking from the bottom
Tear-out
Removing wet materials that can't be dried, such as soaked drywall, insulation and carpet pad, so the structure can dry. Water damage restoration process

W

Water backup coverage
An add-on (endorsement) to a home policy that pays for damage from water or sewage backing up through drains or a failed sump pump. Sewage backup in the basement
Water extraction
Removing standing water with pumps and extractors. It is the fastest way to cut drying time. Water removal and extraction
Water mitigation
Stopping water damage from spreading: extraction, removing wet materials and drying, before repairs start. Water mitigation
Wicking
How water climbs up porous materials like drywall and carpet pad, spreading damage above the waterline. Water stain on the ceiling

X

Xactimate
Estimating software many insurers and contractors use to price repair work line by line. Water damage restoration cost
